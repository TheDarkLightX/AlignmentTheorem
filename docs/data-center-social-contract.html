The Data Center Bargain

A community should not have to choose between rejecting artificial intelligence infrastructure and subsidizing it. The Alignment Theorem suggests a third option: admit only the projects that pay their full costs, protect every modeled household, and share the authenticated surplus.

No cost shifting. No unfunded benefit. No constitutional amendment that silently removes the guarantees.

The debate has changed

Data centers are becoming public-scale infrastructure.

Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory’s 2026 update estimates a 2030 reference case of 649 TWh for U.S. data centers—about 11.8% of national electricity use—with a compounded uncertainty range of 521–843 TWh. FERC is requiring regional grid operators to address cost shifting, transparency, generation adequacy, and flexible large loads. Pennsylvania has moved toward full energy-cost recovery, local approval, public disclosure, environmental protection, and community benefit agreements.

The benefits can be national.

AI capability, corporate value, and national productivity can rise far from the host community.

The burdens are often local.

Substations, transmission, water withdrawals, generator emissions, tonal noise, roads, emergency services, and land-use changes remain physically concentrated.

The accounting can hide the bargain.

A profitable facility can still shift costs to ratepayers or leave the most affected household worse off. Aggregate growth is not a proof of local benefit.

The mathematical constitution

Distribute only the residual that remains after the real obligations.

Let authenticated gross public rent or committed project payment be \(G\). Let \(C\) contain all senior grid, reliability, water, environmental, public-service, security, audit, and decommissioning claims. The distributable reserve is:

\[R=(G-C)_+.\]

For household \(i\), let \(L_i\) be the authenticated project-caused loss, \(B_i\) the direct project benefit that actually reaches that household, and \(m\) the universal resident floor. Define:

\[d_i=(L_i-B_i)_+,\qquad h_i=\max\{m,d_i\}.\]

The transfer \(h_i\) is the pointwise least payment that both gives the household the universal floor and prevents the modeled project burden from making it worse off. Across \(n\) households, the exact least reserve is:

\[H(m)=\sum_{i=1}^n\max\{m,d_i\}=nm+\sum_{i=1}^n(d_i-m)_+.\]
Local admission condition: the cash bargain is funded only when \(G\ge C+H(m)\). A promised compute floor \(c\) is separate and requires \(Q\ge nc\). Compute cannot silently replace cash compensation.
Why the floor matters

An equal dividend can still fail the community.

The smallest counterexample

One household has a project loss of 2, receives no direct benefit, and is promised a universal dividend of 1. The dividend is fully funded—but the household is still worse off by 1. The protective payment is 2, not 1.

The efficient populist structure

Pay everyone the base \(m\). Then add only the top-up \((d_i-m)_+\) to households whose authenticated deficit exceeds the base. After the floor and no-harm reserve are funded, allocate the remaining surplus with a transparent prioritarian rule.

Important: physical limits are vetoes, not prices. A community benefit payment should not buy permission to violate a critical water, reliability, safety, or pollution boundary.
A policy local governments can use

Fourteen moves that convert bargaining power into durable public benefit.

1

Conditional use only

Require special-exception or conditional-use approval above local load, area, water, generator, or expansion thresholds. Do not treat a rapidly changing high-energy facility as an ordinary by-right warehouse.

2

No secret project

Require beneficial ownership, end users, peak MW, water, PUE, WUE, backup power, noise, jobs, incentives, phasing, and infrastructure disclosures before the first discretionary hearing.

3

Growth pays for growth

Make the project fund attributable generation, transmission, distribution, substations, water, roads, emergency services, studies, stranded-cost security, and decommissioning.

4

Hard physical envelopes

Put enforceable peak-load, ramp, water, drought, noise, generator, emissions, lighting, setback, habitat, and safety limits into the permit.

5

Security before operation

Require bankruptcy-resistant escrow, bonds, letters of credit, or parent guarantees sufficient for remediation, unfinished infrastructure, public-benefit nonperformance, and site restoration.

6

Residual-rent agreement

Combine a nonavoidable capacity or concession payment, full senior-cost recovery, and a variable share of audited residual value. Never rely only on accounting profit.

7

Universal benefit + top-ups

Use lawful bill credits, rebates, resident trust distributions, energy assistance, or equivalent broad benefits—then target additional compensation to households with verified deficits above the floor.

8

Distinct host-zone share

Give neighborhoods closest to substations, corridors, cooling, generators, and construction an additional upside share beyond make-whole compensation.

9

Verified load flexibility

Require a tested curtailment amount, response time, duration, rebound limit, emissions boundary, periodic drills, and penalties for nonperformance.

10

Conditional incentives

Make each year of a tax benefit depend on current compliance. Suspend or claw back benefits for broken reporting, job, infrastructure, environmental, or community-benefit commitments.

11

Public evidence and challenge

Use independent meters, machine-readable reports, signed epoch receipts, resident complaints, audit rotation, conflict disclosure, and escrow for contested amounts.

12

Anti-speculation milestones

Expire or charge for unused capacity reservations. Reopen dormant permits, inflated forecasts, serial transfers, and projects that miss financing, construction, or energization milestones.

13

Constitutional review

Require annual review, a major periodic review, and reopening after material expansion or ownership change—without allowing amendments to silently weaken senior claims or hard limits.

14

Regional compact

Coordinate minimum disclosure, bond, water, noise, audit, tariff, and benefit standards so communities do not compete by shifting costs across the same grid and watershed.

Post-AGI economics

Intelligence growth is not yet a deflation theorem.

Let \(a_t\) be effective productivity that actually reaches the household bundle after automation coverage, diffusion, access, and pass-through. Let \(r_t\) be the scarce-resource and infrastructure factor, and \(\mu_t\) the markup and rent-extraction factor.

\[\frac{P_{t+1}}{P_t}=\frac{r_t\mu_t}{a_t}.\]

Prices fall in that period exactly when \(a_t>r_t\mu_t\). Raw model capability can double while diffusion is blocked, energy becomes scarcer, physical bottlenecks remain, or monopoly rents absorb the savings. And even falling prices do not guarantee household abundance if wages and asset ownership fall faster.

The missing link is distribution. The Alignment Theorem does not assume that AGI automatically benefits everyone. It supplies a constitution that can route authenticated surplus into cash, compute, resilience, and constrained household agents while refusing unfunded promises.
Why the full version requires Tau Language

The constitution must be able to reason about changes to itself.

A conventional chain can hold money. A legal document can state duties. Lean can prove theorems. Ordinary software can execute a rule. The full Alignment Theorem needs all of those functions connected to one live semantic constitution: the rules must be formal, decidable, executable, inspectable, and safely revisable under higher-order requirements.

A practical path

Local governments can start before Tau Net is production-ready.

0–30 days

Initiate conditional-use zoning, standard disclosures, applicant-funded independent review, and an inventory of pending applications and grandfathering exposure.

31–180 days

Adopt hard envelopes, bonds, a community-benefit term sheet, clawback rules, public meters and dashboards, a complaint process, and regional minimum standards.

Shadow Tau pilot

Translate every legal obligation into an authenticated Boolean fact. Run the Tau gate beside human administration without moving value. Compare every decision, dispute, appeal, and amendment before production use.

The significance

AGI infrastructure can become a bargain communities actively choose.

The data-center debate is usually framed as acceleration versus obstruction. The Alignment Theorem reframes it as constitutional mechanism design. Build when the project can prove that growth pays for growth, hard limits are respected, the most affected household is protected, broad residents receive a floor, compute promises are real, and the rules cannot be weakened in the dark.

The goal is not to tax intelligence into scarcity. The goal is to make expanding intelligence lower real household scarcity—and to refuse any project that cannot fund the promises used to obtain consent.
