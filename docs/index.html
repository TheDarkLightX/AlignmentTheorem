01
The core insight

Ethics and profit point the same way when the rules are funded.

Policy

Explicit policy

A versioned community policy defines acceptable observable actions. The theorem is policy-relative and does not claim objective moral truth.

Agents

Aligned agents

Human and AI agents can follow ethical constraints while pursuing useful, profitable tasks. Any capable lab may train AI agents against the public policy interface.

Incentives

Finite incentives

Funded rewards and conservative enforcement must exceed bounded deviation gains, compliance costs, and optimizer error by a strict margin.

Gate

Publication gate

A deterministic Tau gate checks authenticated obligations. Rejection pays nothing and leaves the committed reserve unchanged.

02
Version 1 and Version 1.1

Use the concrete V1 mechanism when its premises fit.

V1 ties EETF eligibility to funded rewards and scarcity-linked upside that the excluded branch cannot obtain. The paper’s negative “penalty” term represents that missed upside; it is an opportunity cost and never a tax, fine, burn, confiscation, or balance debit. V1.1 generalizes the same ordering when costs, optimizer error, or relative growth must be explicit.

G(t) < M(t) × [R(t) + L(t)]
V1: L is scarcity upside available only to the eligible branch

V1 exact threshold

For R + L > 0, the least strict integer multiplier is floor(G / (R + L)) + 1. If G = 0, every positive M suffices.

Exclusion is load-bearing

L must be exclusive scarcity upside. If the excluded branch can obtain it, or the upside is common to both choices, L cancels and cannot establish V1.

V1.1 generalization

Define K = R + L and include deviation gain, compliance cost, and optimizer error in B. The finite condition is M(t)K(t) > B(t).

Relative growth

Growing opposing opportunities are covered only when B(t) / K(t) eventually remains below M(t).

Architecture

Ethics-to-economics pipeline

Governance selects an explicit policy. External evidence providers, including meters, oracles, and signed attestations, supply the facts the network cannot derive on its own. Tau checks the policy relationship against those facts, and reserve-backed settlement publishes eligible work.

Receipts inform review and revision

The agent proposes an action and optimizes the task. External evidence providers, including meters, oracles, and signed attestations, supply the facts the network cannot observe on its own. Tau evaluates the declared relation. The settlement layer controls value and publication.

The trust boundary is external. The gate enforces that the rules were followed. It cannot verify that the facts fed into those rules were true. Meters can be miscalibrated, oracles can be bribed, and attestations can be forged; the chain faithfully executes on whatever validly-signed inputs it receives. A production system needs independent evidence providers, dispute mechanisms, and audit rotation. The theorem assumes honest authentication; it does not provide it.

05
Formal proof

The finite incentive argument

Step 1: Fix a versioned policy and an authenticated evidence boundary.
Step 2: Admit publication only when all seven policy obligations hold.
Step 3: Prove that every rejected settlement has zero payout and no reserve effect.
Step 4: Prove that every accepted payout is funded and conserves the reserve.
Step 5: Bound private deviation gain, compliance cost, and optimizer error.
Step 6: Require a strict finite incentive margin over those bounds.
Step 7: Conclude that every epsilon-optimal modeled choice is policy compliant.
∎ QED

The exact statement is machine checked in proofs/v2/AlignmentTheoremV2.lean. The conclusion remains conditional on the declared bounds and authenticated facts.

06
Why this works for AI

Feasible today, bounded by design.

The theorem does not require a hypothetical future agent. It requires four properties that are individually present today, combined with explicit boundaries on what is not proven.

Feasible joint behavior

Present-day assistants show that an agent can pursue a task while following ethical and safety constraints. This establishes behavioral feasibility.

Economic reinforcement

When compliant work pays best, training and profit seeking point agents toward the same observable behavior.

Verifier-conditioned

The agent proposes actions. Authenticated evidence and deterministic policy checks decide what the network can publish and reward.

Explicit limits

The result does not prove universal generalization, objective ethics, honest sensors, coalition safety, or bounds outside the modeled action class.
